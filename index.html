What changed, and why

  1. No photographs of pillars. The brand is called The Foundation Framework — so the visual is a framework, drawn as an architectural section. Three members rising from one foundation, tied at three levels. It is vector, it is yours, nobody else on earth has it, and it weighs 2 KB. The four AI renders of columns are gone.
  2. The motion now means something. The members grow upward from the ground line, one after another, and the tie-lines draw across between them. The page literally builds from the ground up, which is the sentence your own copy opens with. That is worth more than parallax for its own sake.
  3. Type. Inter is out. Instrument Serif for the headlines, Archivo for the text, IBM Plex Mono for labels — and the mono label appears once per section rather than on every element, so it reads as a system instead of a tic.
  4. Your colours, unchanged. #F2EEE5 and #1F3054 are lifted straight from your site. The only addition is a single brass accent used about four times on the whole page.
  5. Every section has a different shape. Hero is asymmetric type plus a figure; then a full-bleed navy statement; then a full-width headline with an offset measure; then a horizontal three-step path. Your current site repeats one 50/50 slab four times, which is the thing that makes it read as a template.
  6. Two bugs fixed. The header now has a real surface, so it is legible wherever it sits — on your live site the logo and nav disappear into the photographs. And there is no horizontal overflow at any width; yours drifts 14px on a 390px phone.
  7. No invented clients. I removed the case studies rather than redraw them. Ember & Bean and the generated meeting-room photos are the fastest way to lose a prospect who checks.

Copy is yours, verbatim, apart from the hero headline — Digital Pillars / The Foundation Framework was set letter-by-letter over a stock sky, which told a visitor your name but not what you do. Swap it back if you prefer; nothing else depends on it.
